### January
*   **Air/Land (Avian):** Peak overwintering season. Tens of thousands of migratory wading birds and wildfowl are fully settled. Key species include Dark-bellied Brent Geese, Dunlin, Oystercatchers, Curlew, Redshank, Grey Plover, and Black-tailed Godwit. (Time span: Nov–Mar).
*   **Sea (Marine):** Grey Seals (*Halichoerus grypus*) pupping season concludes (Late Aug–Jan). Both Harbour (Common) Seals and Grey Seals can be seen hauled out on the mudflats year-round.
*   **Land (Flora):** Flora is largely dormant, though coastal evergreens maintain the landscape.

### February
*   **Air/Land (Avian):** Overwintering Brent Geese and waders remain in large numbers, feeding heavily on eelgrass and saltmarsh vegetation to build reserves for migration. (Time span: Nov–Mar).
*   **Sea (Marine):** National closed season for recreational Bass (*Dicentrarchus labrax*) fishing begins to protect stock. (Time span: Feb 1–Mar 31).

### March
*   **Air/Land (Avian):** Dark-bellied Brent Geese begin their mass departure, migrating back to their breeding grounds in Arctic Siberia. (Time span: Throughout March, dependent on easterly winds).
*   **Land (Flora):** Native woodland/hedgerow wildflowers awaken. Lesser Celandine (*Ficaria verna*) and Wood Anemone (*Anemone nemorosa*) begin blooming. (Time span: Mar–May).
*   **Sea (Marine):** Bass recreational closed season continues.

### April
*   **Air/Land (Avian):** Spring migration begins. Ospreys (*Pandion haliaetus*) occasionally pass through heading north. Common Terns (*Sterna hirundo*) arrive and begin nesting on managed harbour rafts.
*   **Land (Flora):** Early Purple Orchids (*Orchis mascula*) start blooming (Time span: Apr–early Jun). English Bluebells (*Hyacinthoides non-scripta*) bloom in coastal woodlands. Sea Kale (*Crambe maritima*) begins showing white flower heads.
*   **Sea (Marine):** Cuttlefish (*Sepia officinalis*) begin migrating into shallow harbour waters and eelgrass beds to breed and spawn. (Time span: Apr–Aug). Bass nursery area fishing restrictions begin on April 30th.

### May
*   **Air/Land (Avian):** Common Terns are actively nesting and establishing breeding territories.
*   **Land (Flora):** Peak spring bloom. Common Spotted-orchids (*Dactylorhiza fuchsii*) and Bee Orchids (*Ophrys apifera*) begin blooming (Time span: mid-May–Aug). Sea Kale is in full bloom.
*   **Land (Fauna):** Roe Deer (*Capreolus capreolus*) fawns are born in the fields and woodlands surrounding West Wittering. (Time span: May–Jun).
*   **Sea (Marine):** Cuttlefish spawning continues; eggs ("sea grapes") are laid on seaweed.

### June
*   **Sea (Marine):** Harbour (Common) Seal (*Phoca vitulina*) pupping season begins on the harbour mudflats. (Time span: Jun–Aug).
*   **Air/Land (Avian):** Tern breeding season peaks; parents are highly active flying back and forth to feed chicks.
*   **Land (Flora):** Summer coastal flora thrives at East Head and dunes. Pyramidal Orchids (*Anacamptis pyramidalis*) begin blooming (Time span: Jun–mid-Aug). Sea Pinks/Thrift (*Armeria maritima*), Sea Lavender (*Limonium vulgare*), Sea Campion, and Beach Roses are in bloom.

### July
*   **Sea (Marine):** Peak pupping time for Harbour Seals. Cuttlefish adults are present but begin dying off after spawning.
*   **Land (Fauna):** Roe Deer rutting (mating) season begins, with stags becoming highly territorial. (Time span: mid-Jul–mid-Aug).
*   **Land (Flora):** Sea Lavender and Sea Pinks continue blooming. Sea Kale transitions from flowering to producing circular green seed pods. Common Spotted-orchids finish blooming.

### August
*   **Sea (Marine):** Harbour Seal pupping concludes. Grey Seal pupping season begins in the wider region. (Time span: Late Aug–Jan).
*   **Land (Fauna):** Roe Deer rutting ends.
*   **Land (Flora):** Pyramidal Orchids finish blooming. Blackberries (brambles) begin ripening across coastal hedgerows for foraging. Many coastal plants shift to setting seed.

### September
*   **Air/Land (Avian):** Autumn migration starts. "Early bird" arrivals of Dark-bellied Brent Geese and returning waders (Dunlin, Redshank, Curlew) begin populating the harbour for winter. (Time span: Sep–Oct arrivals).
*   **Land (Flora):** Blackberries reach peak ripeness. Oak trees (*Quercus robur*) begin dropping mature acorns (fruiting). Common Heather (*Calluna vulgaris*) provides late-season nectar.
*   **Sea (Marine):** Grey Seal pupping season continues.

### October
*   **Air/Land (Avian):** The primary influx of overwintering Dark-bellied Brent Geese occurs, dramatically increasing the harbour bird populations.
*   **Land (Flora):** Acorn drop from Oak trees peaks. Landscape transitions to autumn foliage.
*   **Sea (Marine):** Grey Seal pupping continues.

### November
*   **Air/Land (Avian):** Winter flocks of migratory waders and waterfowl are fully established. Peak birdwatching season begins.
*   **Sea (Marine):** Bass nursery area boat fishing restrictions end on Nov 1st. Grey Seal pupping continues.

### December
*   **Air/Land (Avian):** Huge flocks of Brent Geese and overwintering waders are fully established, feeding on exposed mudflats at low tide.
*   **Sea (Marine):** Grey Seal pupping continues. Harbour Seals rest year-round on mudflats.
*   **Land (Flora):** Winter dormancy; local wildlife relies on remaining seeds and berries.
